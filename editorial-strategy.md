## 1. BRAND ONBOARDING SUMMARY

Skin Lab Editorial is an ingredient-first skincare intelligence platform on Instagram and TikTok. It is not a beauty brand. It is not an influencer channel. It is a publication — a scientific journal crossed with a European editorial magazine, operating in a space where most content exists to sell rather than to inform.

What makes it different: we read the research before the audience does. We parse the INCI list, quantify the percentages, and follow the mechanism from the lab to the label. We then deliver a verdict — not a suggestion, a verdict — written with the precision and restraint of someone who has earned the authority to have an opinion.

The ship test, in practice, means this: before any post is published, ask whether a reader would mistake it for an advertisement. If the answer is yes, or even maybe, it does not ship. Every editorial decision — angle, copy, color, image type, call to action — is made through this filter. We would rather publish nothing than publish something promotional.

## 2. AUDIENCE ANALYSIS — INSTAGRAM vs. TIKTOK

### Instagram Audience

**Demographics:** Women 25–42, educated, European and Americas urban centers (London, Paris, New York, Barcelona, Amsterdam). Household income upper-middle to high. Likely employed in knowledge-work fields: medicine, law, design, finance, academia, tech.

**Psychographics:** She has been burned by skincare marketing. She bought the serum based on a campaign and felt nothing. She now reads labels, tracks ingredients, and is skeptical of anyone claiming authority without evidence. She follows science-adjacent accounts but finds them either too clinical or too dull. She reads *The Gentlewoman*, owns books about design and food, and does not identify with the influencer beauty world. She is not looking for trends — she is looking for clarity.

**What she comes to Instagram for:** Deep reference material. She wants something she can save, return to, and trust. A carousel she screenshots. A label analysis she shows her dermatologist. She is not scrolling passively — she is curating her knowledge.

**What triggers a save:** A post that teaches her something she didn't know but immediately recognizes as true. Precise percentages. Specific mechanisms. A verdict she agrees with but couldn't have articulated herself.

**What triggers a share:** When a post says exactly what she's been thinking but couldn't prove. The provocation format. A label reveal that validates her skepticism about an overpriced formulation.

**What triggers a follow:** Consistency of voice and authority over 3–5 posts. She does not follow after one post — she vets. She checks the grid. The account needs to look like a publication before she commits.

**What fails for this audience:** Anything that looks like an ad. Lifestyle imagery. Overuse of superlatives. Brand enthusiasm that hasn't been earned through analysis. Educational content that oversimplifies. Content that talks down to her. Anything with a CTA like "drop a comment below" or "tag a friend."

---

### TikTok Audience

**Demographics:** Women 22–35, skewed slightly younger and broader geographically than Instagram. More diverse income range — she is earlier in her skincare education journey but already past beginner status. She knows retinol exists but doesn't fully understand retinaldehyde vs. retinyl esters. She wants to get smarter, fast.

**Psychographics:** She is discovering that the skincare industry has misled her and she is genuinely angry about it — in a productive, curious way. She follows dermatologists and chemistry-adjacent creators. She values "tell me something the brands don't want me to know" content. She is more impulse-driven in how she consumes content but still saves for reference.

**What she comes to TikTok for:** Discovery and speed. The concept in 15 seconds. The answer she didn't know she needed. She wants to feel smarter after 30 seconds than she was before.

**What triggers a save:** The myth-busting slide. The price comparison reveal. The "you've been using this wrong" moment.

**What triggers a share:** When a post makes her feel like she caught an industry in a lie. The provocation opener. The Worth The Price? price-gap reveal.

**What triggers a follow:** A strong first impression from a single post, but she will cross-check Instagram before committing. TikTok is where she discovers; Instagram is where she decides to trust.

**What fails for this audience:** Slowness. More than 5 slides before the payoff. A tone that sounds like a textbook. Anything that requires her to already know something to understand the opening. Over-designed slides that feel corporate.

---

### Platform Relationship Summary

Instagram is the archive. TikTok is the discovery engine. Every week, TikTok draws in a reader who has never heard of Skin Lab Editorial — the Instagram account converts her into a committed follower. TikTok should always feel like a compressed, punchy extract of the deeper Instagram content. The clearest signal of a working system: profile visits on Instagram spike within 48 hours of a TikTok post.

## 3. COLUMN SYSTEM — DEFINITIVE SPEC

### Column 1 — INGREDIENT SCHOOL
**Tagline:** "X, Exactly." — The full mechanism, no mythology.
**Promise to the reader:** Everything you've heard about this ingredient, evaluated against what the evidence actually says.

**Visual identity:**
- Cover color: Lab Sage (#BBD1C6) background, Ink text
- Fraunces placement: the ingredient name, large, at marquee scale — this is the one word that gets the spotlight
- Interior slides: rotate Bone → Ink → Bone → Lab Mist → Bone → Ink → Lab Frost (no two Ink adjacent)
- Photo type: Texture macro (formula close-up) on Bone bg for the swatch slide; Lab objects for mechanism illustration

**Cover treatment:** Lab Sage full-bleed, ingredient name in Fraunces at maximum scale, "INGREDIENT SCHOOL" label in Proxima Nova Light tracked caps below. Column name is a recurring anchor the reader learns to recognize.

**Platform fit:** Instagram primary (full 9-slide carousel), TikTok secondary (compressed 4-5 slide version)
- Instagram: this is the column that gets saved most. It is reference material.
- TikTok: myth → mechanism → label recommendation → end card. Optimized for discovery.

**Posting frequency:** 1× per week (the backbone of the editorial calendar)

**KPI goal:** Saves first. This column should consistently outperform the account save rate benchmark (>3% of reach). Secondary: comment quality — ingredient-specific questions (score 2-3) indicate the right audience is engaging.

**Tone within brand voice:** The most textbook-adjacent of the six columns, but never dry. Precision and warmth coexist. The reader should feel like a knowledgeable friend explained this over coffee — not that they read a Wikipedia entry.

**3 additional example titles:**
- Bakuchiol, Exactly.
- Azelaic Acid, Exactly.
- Ceramides, Exactly.

**KPI goal:** Trust-building. The primary metric is comment quality — this column should generate the highest proportion of score-2 and score-3 comments (formulation-specific questions, expert engagement). Secondary: shares, because this content prompts "have you seen this?" behavior in the audience.

**Tone within brand voice:** The most forensic of the six columns. Precise, calm, no enthusiasm for enthusiasm's sake. The tone is that of someone reading a document aloud and explaining each clause — a legal analyst, not a reviewer. When the formulation is good, we say so without drama. When it isn't, we explain why without accusation.

**Platform fit:** TikTok primary — this is the most scroll-stopping, shareable format in the system. Instagram secondary (more detailed analysis). The price gap is the hook; TikTok delivers it first.

If the comment section fills with "which one should I buy?" rather than formulation questions, the hook is working for reach but may not be building the right audience.

**Tone within brand voice:** The most direct and urgent of the six columns. Shorter sentences. Less hedging in the verdict. But still no drama, no outrage, no "you've been scammed." The tone is: here is the evidence. Consider it.

### Column 5 — BRAND STUDY
**Tagline:** "Understanding [Brand]."
**Promise to the reader:** Not a product review — a full editorial on what a brand actually is: its formulation philosophy, its hero ingredients, its claims versus its evidence, and what it's actually selling.

**Platform fit:** Instagram primary and primary only. The Brand Study is too long and too nuanced for TikTok compression. A single key insight from the study can become a TikTok, but the study itself lives on Instagram.

**Posting frequency:** 1× per month

**KPI goal:** PR attraction. This column exists to demonstrate to brands that Skin Lab Editorial is a credible, thorough, independent voice — not to drive saves or follows (though it does). The primary goal is that brand's PR team reads it and reaches out. Secondary: engagement from the most educated segment of the audience.

**Tone within brand voice:** The most analytical and long-form. The closest to traditional editorial criticism. We are evaluating a brand the way a book review evaluates an author — comprehensively, with context, with judgment, but without hostility.

### Column 6 — OPEN / PROVOCATION
**Tagline:** A statement we believe and will defend.
**Promise to the reader:** An opinion — argued, evidenced, concluded. We take a position on something the industry avoids.

**Cover treatment:** Ink bg full-bleed. The provocation statement in Fraunces — as large as the text permits. Nothing else on the cover except the column label in Proxima Nova Light tracked at minimum size. Maximum restraint = maximum impact.

**Platform fit:** Both platforms equally. This is the column that earns new followers on TikTok (reaches people who don't yet know the account) and deepens loyalty on Instagram.

**Posting frequency:** 1× per week (but these can be shorter production cycles — single-image posts or 3-slide carousels)

**KPI goal:** Reach and follows. This is the column that grows the audience. The metric is follow conversion rate — how many profile visits converted to follows after this post. Secondary: shares, because a good provocation gets forwarded.

**Tone within brand voice:** The most editorial-opinionated. Still no drama, no outrage, no exclamation marks. The tone is: this is what we believe, here is why, here is the evidence, here is where it leads. The reader may disagree — that is acceptable, and even productive. We do not write to be agreed with; we write to be thought about.

**3 additional example titles:**

## 4. CONTENT FREQUENCY & CALENDAR LOGIC

### Posts Per Week (Launch Phase — Months 1–3)

**Instagram:** 3 posts/week
**TikTok:** 3 posts/week
**Total:** 6 posts/week, approximately 24 posts/month across both platforms

This is the minimum viable frequency to establish a publication rhythm. Below 3 posts/week on Instagram, the grid does not build fast enough for new visitors to read the account as a publication. Above 4/week in the launch phase, production quality suffers.

---

### Column Rotation Logic

**Primary rules:**
1. Never the same column twice in a row on the same platform
2. Never two Ink-dominant covers adjacent in the Instagram grid (Worth The Price? and Open/Provocation both use Ink — separate them by at least 2 posts)
3. Ingredient School anchors the week — it is always present, every week
4. Open/Provocation opens the week (Monday/Tuesday) — it sets the editorial tone
5. The most research-intensive column (The Verdict, Brand Study) lands mid-week when attention is highest

**Weekly structure (Instagram):**
- Tuesday: Open/Provocation or The Label (lighter production, sets the week)
- Thursday: Ingredient School (anchor post — always Thursday, 09:00–11:00 CET)
- Sunday: The Verdict or Worth The Price? (higher production, slower-scroll Sunday)

**Weekly structure (TikTok):**
- Wednesday: Ingredient School compressed version (same week's IG content)
- Friday: Worth The Price? or Open/Provocation (high-share potential for weekend)
- Saturday: Cross-platform — can reshare compressed version of Sunday's upcoming IG post as a preview

---

### Column Balance Across a Month

A full month at standard frequency (12 IG posts, 12 TikTok posts):

**Instagram (12 posts/month):**
- Ingredient School: 4 posts (1/week — the backbone)
- The Label: 2–3 posts
- Open/Provocation: 2–3 posts
- The Verdict: 1–2 posts
- Worth The Price?: 1 post
- Brand Study: 1 post

**TikTok (12 posts/month):**
- Ingredient School compressed: 4 posts
- Worth The Price?: 3–4 posts (this column outperforms on TikTok)
- Open/Provocation: 3–4 posts
- The Verdict compressed: 1 post

This matches the Community Manager's content ratio target:
- 40% Ingredient/Label deep dives: covered by Ingredient School + The Label
- 20% Product reviews: covered by The Verdict + Worth The Price?
- 15% Brand Studies + thought leadership: Brand Study + editorial Open posts
- 15% Myth-busting: lives inside Ingredient School and Open/Provocation
- 10% Community/opinion: Open/Provocation

---

### The Narrative Arc of a Month

**Week 1 — Establish authority.** Open with a provocation that signals editorial conviction. Follow with the first Ingredient School (the most foundational ingredient in the campaign). End the week with The Label — a product the provocation has implicitly put into context.

**Week 2 — Build trust through depth.** The Verdict (full editorial review) arrives. The audience has now seen a provocation, an ingredient analysis, and a label — now they see a full review and understand the system. Second Ingredient School reinforces the pattern.

**Week 3 — Challenge assumptions.** Worth The Price? arrives — this is the week designed for reach and TikTok virality. Pair with a second The Label that extends the week's theme. Third Ingredient School.

**Week 4 — Consolidate and contextualize.** Brand Study (if included this month) — the most ambitious piece. Final Ingredient School. Optional: a second Open/Provocation that synthesizes the month's themes into a closing editorial statement.

---

### How TikTok Relates to Instagram Each Week

**Default relationship: companion, 2–3 days later, compressed.**

The TikTok version of an Ingredient School post drops 2–3 days after the Instagram carousel. This is intentional — it creates a discovery pathway. A viewer encounters the TikTok, saves it or visits the profile, and is directed to Instagram for the full version. The TikTok caption explicitly references Instagram ("Full breakdown on Instagram").

**Worth The Price? is the exception:** TikTok-first, same day or one day before Instagram. The price gap reveal is a TikTok-native hook. The Instagram version (more detailed INCI analysis) follows and deepens what TikTok started.

**Open/Provocation:** Same-day cross-post. The statement works equally on both platforms. The Instagram version may be a carousel (statement + argument + evidence + takeaway). The TikTok version is 3 slides maximum — statement → evidence → verdict.

**Brand Study:** Instagram only, no TikTok version. A single key finding from the study can become a standalone TikTok at a later date, framed as a discrete insight rather than a summary.

The skincare industry has designed its marketing to trigger emotional purchases disconnected from skin biology. The real story is: the decision architecture the audience has been operating inside is not designed for their benefit.
**The ONE thing the audience should feel:** Validated. They already suspected this. We are confirming what they knew.

### Post 04 — Retinal, Exactly.

**Column:** Ingredient School (confirmed)
**Platform:** Instagram primary (9 slides), TikTok secondary (4 slides)
**Editorial angle:** Retinal (retinaldehyde) is not the same as retinol. This is the most important distinction in the retinoid category and it is systematically underexplained, including by brands that formulate with it. The real story is: the conversion step between retinol and retinoic acid is where most efficacy is lost — and retinaldehyde skips it. This is a genuine mechanistic advantage that the audience deserves to understand.
**The ONE thing the audience should feel:** The satisfaction of a confusion resolved. They've seen "retinal" and "retinol" used interchangeably; now the distinction is permanent.
**Suggested slide count:** 9 slides (Cover → The retinoid conversion pathway, simplified → Why retinaldehyde is different → Efficacy evidence → Concentration and stability → Why it's harder to formulate → What to look for on a label → Texture/swatch (no text) → Verdict)
**Brand/product considerations:** Medik8 Crystal Retinal is the logical reference product for this column — do not review it here (that belongs to Post 05, The Verdict). Reference Medik8 as a formulation example where the science is applied correctly, but keep the post ingredient-focused.
**PR_FLAG: NO** — Ingredient education, no brand as primary subject.
**DIST_FLAG: NO** — Standard Thursday cadence.
**Priority:** Week 2 — positions retinaldehyde before The Verdict on Medik8 Crystal Retinal (Post 05) lands the following week. This sequencing is intentional: the audience understands the mechanism before they read the review.

## 7. ANALYTICS STRATEGY

### The 3 Metrics to Obsess Over in Month 1

**1. Save rate per post (saves ÷ reach)**
This is the primary quality signal. Benchmark: >3% is strong for the niche. Anything consistently above 5% is an exceptional post. Track this per column to understand which educational formats are being trusted as reference material. A post with 1,000 saves and 30,000 reach is a better post than one with 300 saves and 100,000 reach — for this brand.

**2. Follow conversion rate from provocation posts**
Open/Provocation posts drive follows at a higher rate than any other column — if they're working. Track: how many profile visits came from this post, and what percentage converted to follows. If the conversion rate is low (visits but no follows), the grid or the visual system is failing to convert the interest the provocation generated. This tells us whether the first impression of the account matches the quality of the content that attracted the visit.

**3. TikTok → Instagram profile visits (within 48 hours of TikTok post)**
This is the pipeline metric. TikTok is the discovery engine; Instagram is the archive. If TikTok posts are not generating Instagram profile visits, the cross-platform ecosystem is not functioning. Track: in the 48 hours after each TikTok post, how many new Instagram profile visits and follows arrived. This tells us whether the TikTok content is doing its job.

---

### How to Know If a Column Is Working

**Ingredient School:** Strong if save rate >4% consistently. Secondary check: are comments including specific formulation questions (concentration, molecular weight, INCI-specific)? Score-2 and score-3 comments (on the analytics agent scale) should make up >40% of the comment section.

**The Label:** Strong if share rate is elevated (above-average shares-to-stories for the account) and if comments reference the specific product or ingredient named. Weak signal: lots of saves but no comments — this may indicate the content was found but didn't trigger a reaction.

**The Verdict:** Strong if it generates PR contact (email, DM, or comment from the brand's team) within 7 days. Secondary: save rate >3% and comments that engage with the verdict specifically ("I disagree with the Lab Score on X because..."). Disagreement is a quality signal.

**Worth The Price?:** Strong if TikTok reach exceeds Instagram reach for the same content by 3×. If this ratio isn't met, the hook is not sharp enough for TikTok. Also: shares-to-stories should be the highest of all column types.

**Brand Study:** Strong if the brand's marketing or PR team responds (the explicit KPI is PR attraction). Secondary: the post is referenced or quoted by other accounts, which signals authority. Comments from brand employees are also a strong signal.

**Open/Provocation:** Strong if follow conversion rate from this post exceeds 1% (1 new follower per 100 profile visits). Secondary: shares. Weak signal: lots of likes but no follows and no shares — the content is being acknowledged but not trusted enough to follow or forward.

---

### How to Know If a Post Failed (and What to Do)

**A post has failed when:**
- Save rate <1% AND comment section shows <20% score-1 or higher comments
- Follow conversion is below 0.2% (fewer than 1 new follower per 500 profile visits)
- TikTok post generates no Instagram profile visits within 48 hours

**What to do:**
1. Diagnose before changing anything. Was it the hook (the cover slide failed)? Was it the timing? Was it the topic genuinely not resonating, or was the execution wrong?
2. If the cover failed: the post topic may have been right but the cover headline was too obscure or too gentle. Test a more direct cover statement on the next post in the same column.
3. If the topic genuinely didn't resonate: note it and do not repeat that topic angle. The Ingredient School column can revisit an ingredient from a different angle (e.g., "Hyaluronic Acid, Exactly" performed well but a post on hydration mechanisms broadly did not — adjust the frame, not the column).
4. Never delete a post that underperformed. It remains part of the editorial record. Analyze it; do not erase it.
5. Do not pivot strategy on one underperforming post. The threshold for strategic concern is three consecutive underperforming posts within the same column.

---

### When to Add a New Column vs. Retire an Underperforming One

**Add a new column when:**
- A recurring topic type is generating strong engagement but doesn't fit any existing column cleanly
- The account has reached month 3 and the audience is asking a category of question that isn't addressed by the current column set
- A clear editorial opportunity exists that requires a distinct visual and tone treatment
- Rule: do not add a column before completing 3 full cycles of each existing column. The system needs time to establish recognition patterns.

**Retire a column when:**
- Three consecutive posts in the column have underperformed (save rate <1.5% each)
- AND comment quality has not improved
- AND the topic is being addressed better by another column
- Rule: do not retire Ingredient School or The Label in the first 6 months — these are the editorial backbone. If they underperform, the problem is execution, not the column concept.

**Never retire a column after one bad month.** Platforms have algorithmic fluctuations. Two months of consistent underperformance, with post-level diagnosis, is the threshold.

## 8. THE ANALYTICS AGENT — ROLE IN THE SYSTEM

### When the Agent Activates

**Weekly trigger: every Monday morning.** The Analytics & Impact Analyst produces a Week N Performance Report every Monday before 10:00 CET, covering the previous week's posts. This report goes directly to the Community Manager and the CEO.

**Monthly trigger: first Monday of the new month.** Full column performance breakdown, recommendation for the coming month's editorial mix, and any flags for brand-dilution risk or unexpected PR engagement.

---

### What Data the Founder Provides

The system currently operates on manually-provided data, as Instagram and TikTok analytics are not directly accessible via API in this environment. The founder (or designated team member) provides weekly:

**Instagram (per post):**
- Saves, Likes, Comments, Shares
- Reach (impressions from followers vs. non-followers if available)
- Profile visits attributed to each post (available in Instagram Insights)
- Any notable comments worth flagging (quality scoring requires human review)

**TikTok (per post):**
- Views, Saves, Shares, Comments
- Profile visits within 48 hours of post (manual check: Instagram Insights → new followers → note if spike aligns with TikTok post timing)

**Format:** The founder delivers this data as a simple table in a message to the Analytics agent. The agent does not require a specific format — it will extract what it needs. A simple "Post [X]: X saves, X reach, X comments, X shares" per post is sufficient.

---

### What the Agent Does With the Data

1. **Calculates save rate** (saves ÷ reach) per post and tracks against the >3% benchmark
2. **Scores comment quality** based on keywords (formulation-specific language = score 2-3; generic = score 0)
3. **Identifies the week's top and bottom performer** with a specific diagnosis of why
4. **Tracks column performance over time** — which columns are trending up, which are plateauing
5. **Flags any posts that over-indexed on reach but under-indexed on saves** as potential brand-dilution signals
6. **Produces the standard weekly report** in the format defined in the agent file, saved to `/analytics/week-N-report.md`
7. **Issues a single editorial calendar recommendation** — one specific action for the Community Manager to take in the coming week (e.g., "Ingredient School save rate dropped below 3% for the second consecutive week — consider adjusting the cover headline format on the next post to be more direct")

---

### How Analytics Feeds Back Into Editorial Decisions

The feedback loop is tight and explicit:

**Community Manager receives the Monday report** and uses it to brief the CEO for that week's posts. Any recommendation from the analytics agent is treated as a briefing input, not a directive — the editorial team retains creative judgment.

**CEO receives the report** and uses it to calibrate the editorial angle on the current week's posts. If Ingredient School saves are strong, the CEO may brief the Copywriter to lean further into the mechanism-focused structure. If Open/Provocation isn't converting follows, the CEO may brief for a sharper provocation on the next open post.

**Monthly review shapes the calendar.** The Community Manager uses the monthly report to adjust the column frequency in the following month's MASTER-CALENDAR. If Worth The Price? consistently drives the highest TikTok reach, it may move from 1× per 2 weeks to 2× per month. If Brand Study generates no PR response after two consecutive posts, the team reassesses the target brands and the outreach strategy.

**The analytics agent does not make editorial decisions.** It surfaces evidence. The editorial team decides what to do with it. This is by design — it mirrors the brand's own relationship with its audience: we present the evidence; the reader makes the call.
