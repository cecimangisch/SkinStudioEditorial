01 Identity

One word: discernment.

We do the reading, the parsing, and the second-guessing — so you can make one informed decision instead of ten confused ones. Skin LAB Editorial covers skincare the way a great magazine covers culture: with intelligence, taste, and a point of view. We publish verdicts, not advertisements. We cite percentages, not adjectives.

EssenceDiscernment. To see clearly despite the noise.
PersonaA Parisian dermatologist who reads The Gentlewoman and has a studio in the Marais.
AudienceEducated, skeptical readers who have outgrown the noise — they know niacinamide from panthenol.
GoalAttract, inform, retain — then convert credibility into PR partnerships.
Identity lineThe intelligence of a lab. The eye of a magazine.
The three pillars
01

Earned clarity

We make complexity legible without making it simple. We start with the evidence and cut everything else. Our readers want to be trusted with the actual answer.

02

Editorial conviction

We have a point of view, and we defend it. We pick the better retinol. We call out the misleading claim. Opinion is not bias — it is what separates a trusted voice from an aggregator.

03

Quiet luxury of knowledge

Intelligence is the only status signal we care about. Restraint, precision, and earned authority are our luxury markers. We do not chase virality.

The manifesto

Most skincare content is made to sell you something. We are not selling anything.

We read the research. We parse the percentages. We follow the ingredient from the lab to the label to the shelf — and we tell you exactly what we found.

Not what a brand paid to have us say. Not what an algorithm rewarded last week. What the evidence says.

We exist for the moment after the confusion. The moment someone wants an actual answer.

Official isotype · the molecule

The four-node molecule, standing alone.

The isotype is the brand's most reduced mark — the seal's molecule without the text ring. Use it where even the seal is too detailed: app icon at small sizes, favicon, embossing, watermark, a single-colour stamp on packaging. The textured, hand-inked rendering is intentional — it keeps the science human, never sterile.

Reproduce in Ink on a light field, or knock out in Bone/Frost on Ink or Sage. Keep clear space equal to the diameter of one small node on every side.

The seal · molecular stamp · avatar & small uses
When to use the seal

The seal carries Ingredients, decoded on its lower arc. Use it as the Instagram & TikTok avatar, the TikTok end card, story stickers, and any context too small for the full lockup. It is the highest-frequency brand impression — protect its clear space.

The molecular pattern · derived from the seal
How to use the pattern

A quiet, tone-on-tone texture lifted from the seal's molecule. Backgrounds only — story panels, cover fills, the TikTok end card, packaging tissue. Keep it barely there: it must never compete with type or photography, and text never sits directly on the dense version. Two colourways (Sage, Bone/Cloud), two densities. The web utility is .bg-molecule.

Botanical illustration is not a core image type — it pulls toward “natural / herbal / wellness,” which we are not. Use it only as a rare exception for a genuine ingredient-origin story, and only in scientific herbarium-plate style (specimen + Latin binomial), never romantic or floral.

06 Voice

Short sentences. One idea. Full stop.

We write the way a trusted specialist talks — economical, certain, never performing. Active voice. No filler. Percentages when we discuss formulas. We disclose gifted or PR product in the first line, every time.

Do

"Retinaldehyde 0.05%. One conversion step from active. Faster than retinol, gentler than tretinoin. Gifted by the brand — opinion is ours."

Never

"amazing" · "obsessed" · "holy grail" · "game-changer" · exclamation marks · hedging.

Words we own — use consistently
FormulationNot "product." We start with ingredients, not packaging.
MechanismHow an ingredient works at the cellular level. Rigor, not magic.
EfficacyResults measured against evidence. Replaces the empty "effective."
RatioPercentages, concentrations, actives-to-base. Skincare is chemistry.
ConsiderAn invitation to think, not a command to buy.
DiscernTo see clearly despite noise. Our reader's defining act.

08.1 The Lab Score, defined

Three verdicts. One method. Evidence first — then we choose.

Every final call is scored on three axes: efficacy (do the actives work, at a dose that matters), evidence (does the research support the claim), and price-to-active (is the formulation worth what it costs). The verdict is decided before a brand is ever contacted — gifting never moves the score.

Skip no

The formulation does not deliver. Actives below an effective threshold, poor delivery or stability, irritants or fragrance that sabotage the purpose, or a claim the evidence won't support.

Not worth your money at any price.

Save yes, but

A good formulation with a condition. It earns merit, but the purchase is qualified. Save applies when one of these is true:

  • Price — sound formula, but a rival offers better price-to-active. Buy it on a drop.
  • Conditional — effective for a specific skin type or goal, not universal.
  • Redundant — good, but it doesn't beat what you likely already own.
  • Unproven — promising, but the evidence or maturity isn't there yet. Worth watching.
Invest yes

Excellent, and it justifies its price. We would buy it and recommend it without asterisks.

Proven merit, broad benefit, correct price-to-active.

The line that separates them

Skip fails on efficacy or evidence. Invest wins on all three axes. Save is the "yes, but" — a real formulation held back by one condition: price, skin-type fit, redundancy, or evidence still pending. Every final-call slide should carry the method line: scored on formulation, evidence, and price-per-active.
