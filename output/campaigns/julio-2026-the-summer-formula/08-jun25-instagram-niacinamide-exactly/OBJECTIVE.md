## EDITORIAL ANGLE (CEO)

Niacinamide is one of skincare's most marketed ingredients and most misrepresented ones. The 10% concentration is a marketing convention, not a clinical threshold. The actual clinical evidence centers on 5%. Summer relevance is threefold: melanin transfer inhibition (UV increases transfer rate), sebum regulation (not stripping), and barrier support (TEWL reduction). This post establishes Skin Lab as the authority that corrects the market's messaging — not with opinion, but with the published research.

The "Exactly." column format: definition, mechanism, clinical evidence, concentration reality, what to pair with it, what the label should say, summer relevance, and verdict.

**What the reader must feel:** That they now know this ingredient in a way the brand's marketing never allowed them to. Saves are the primary outcome.

## EDITOR IN CHIEF AUDIT NOTES

The 5% vs 10% concentration claim must be handled precisely — state that 5% is where the clinical evidence sits, and that 10% is a market positioning number. Do not say 10% is ineffective or harmful — the evidence is that additional benefit above 5% is not well-established, not that 10% is problematic. Accuracy over provocation.
