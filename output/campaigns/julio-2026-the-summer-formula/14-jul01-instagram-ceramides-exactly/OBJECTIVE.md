## EDITORIAL ANGLE (CEO)

Ceramides appear on approximately half of all barrier-repair product labels and are understood by almost none of the buyers reading them. This post corrects that. The Exactly. column format requires a complete technical account: what ceramides are chemically (sphingolipids), their structural role (lamellar bilayer integrity), the consequence of UV depletion (TEWL, barrier permeability), the specific types to identify on labels (CER NP/AP/EOP), and why humectants alone cannot substitute for ceramide replenishment.

The summer campaign angle: UV degrades ceramides directly. This is why barrier repair is a summer concern, not just a winter one.

**What the reader must feel:** A complete, authoritative understanding. Saves because this functions as a reference document.

## EDITOR IN CHIEF AUDIT NOTES

The "12 types" claim refers to INCI classification. State it as a classification fact, not a functional claim. The humectants-vs-ceramides distinction is critical: humectants attract water (hydration), ceramides restore barrier structure. Both are needed. Do not position one as superior — position them as doing different jobs.
