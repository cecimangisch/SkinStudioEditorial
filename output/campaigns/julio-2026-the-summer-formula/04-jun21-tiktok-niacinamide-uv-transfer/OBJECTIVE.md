## EDITORIAL ANGLE (CEO)

Niacinamide is among the most recognised active ingredients in skincare. Most readers already know it "reduces pigmentation." Almost none know the mechanism: niacinamide does not inhibit melanin synthesis. It inhibits the transfer of melanosomes from melanocytes to keratinocytes. The pigmentation forms — it just does not reach the surface layer where it becomes visible.

This post gives the FYP audience the mechanism they have never been given. The summer angle: UV radiation stimulates melanosome production. Niacinamide is therefore most relevant in the season when melanogenesis is most active.

**What the reader must feel:** Specific curiosity about a mechanism they thought they understood. A desire to save for future reference.
