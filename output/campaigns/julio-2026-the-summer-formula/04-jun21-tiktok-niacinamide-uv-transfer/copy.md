## TIKTOK CAPTION

Niacinamide doesn't stop pigmentation forming. It stops the transfer to the surface layer of skin.

Melanin is produced in melanocytes — that part niacinamide doesn't touch. What it inhibits is the transport of melanosomes from melanocytes to keratinocytes. That is the mechanism. That is what becomes visible pigmentation.

Summer relevance: UV radiation increases melanosome production. Niacinamide is most effective when that transfer rate is highest.

SPF addresses the UV stimulus. Niacinamide addresses the transfer. These are different mechanisms. Both matter.
